Two-factor sign-in means a stolen password alone isn't enough: you also type a code from an app on your phone. Admins must use it. For everyone else it's optional, and recommended.

## Set it up

1. Install an authenticator app on your phone, such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or 1Password.
2. Open [My account](/account) and press **Set up two-factor**. (Admins are taken there at their first sign-in.)
3. Scan the QR code with the app. If you can't scan it, type the key shown under it into the app instead.
4. Type the 6-digit code the app shows and press **Turn on**.
5. **Save your recovery codes.** Press **Copy** or **Download** and keep them somewhere safe, like a password manager or a printed copy in the office safe. You won't see them again.
6. Press **I've saved my codes. Continue**.

## New phone

Sign in (with a recovery code if the old phone is gone), press **Turn off** on My account, then press **Set up two-factor** and scan the code with the new phone. You get new recovery codes too.

Admins can't turn two-factor off: ask another admin to reset your two-factor sign-in, then set it up again with the new phone when you next sign in.

## Make new recovery codes

On My account, press **New recovery codes** and enter your password. The old codes stop working.

## Turn it off

On My account, press **Turn off** and enter your password. Admins can't turn it off. Every admin is told when someone turns off their own two-factor sign-in.

> Warning: Never give your 6-digit code or your recovery codes to anyone, even someone who says they're from the office. If someone asks, tell an admin.
